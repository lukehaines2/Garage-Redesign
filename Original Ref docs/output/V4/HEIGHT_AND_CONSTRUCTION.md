# V4 height and construction-drawing decisions

Researched 23 September 2026 in response to S17. Site: Billeaford Hall, Sloe Lane, Knodishall, IP17 1UU. These are national rules and project implications; this note does not establish that this particular site has permitted-development rights.

## Height answer

For an eligible detached domestic outbuilding in England with a dual-pitched roof, the Class E maximum overall height is **4.0 m**, with **2.5 m maximum eaves**. If any part is within 2 m of the house's curtilage boundary, the entire building has a **2.5 m overall limit**. There is no 4.2 m allowance in these rules. Include projecting parts when checking proximity; the wall line alone is insufficient. [Government technical guidance, Class E E.1(e)-(f)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/permitted-development-rights-for-householders-technical-guidance/permitted-development-rights-for-householders-technical-guidance#class-e-buildings-etc).

On sloping ground, the guidance measures height from the highest ground immediately adjacent to the building to its highest point. The reported 300-400 mm courtyard fall is not a measured datum at the proposed footprint. Record existing levels around that footprint and the proposed floor/ground levels; do not simply subtract 400 mm from a model height. Curtilage can be smaller than the whole estate. [Government guidance, General Issues and Class E](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/permitted-development-rights-for-householders-technical-guidance/permitted-development-rights-for-householders-technical-guidance).

Height alone does not establish eligibility. Check lawful domestic curtilage, the house's planning history/conditions, principal elevation, cumulative building coverage, listed-building/designated-land status and any removal of permitted-development rights. A replacement is not automatically exempt. If permitted development is available, a proposed lawful-development certificate can provide formal confirmation; otherwise assess a planning application. [Planning Portal outbuildings guidance](https://www.planningportal.co.uk/permission/common-projects/outbuildings/planning-permission/), [government introduction](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/permitted-development-rights-for-householders-technical-guidance/permitted-development-rights-for-householders-technical-guidance#introduction).

## Effect on this design

- Dad confirms a 9000 x 6000 mm proposal, equal bays, full dividing wall and an east-facing entrance. These are design decisions, not survey verification. Use equal nominal 3000 mm divisions initially; actual clear spans depend on wall/post dimension conventions and structural detailing.
- The old P02 ridge of 4163 mm is 163 mm above a 4000 mm envelope **if measured from the same relevant datum**. The provisional model datum is not yet related to surveyed ground, so it cannot establish compliance.
- With a simple symmetrical 6000 mm span and 2300 mm eaves, a 30-degree pitch gives approximately 4032 mm ridge. A 4000 mm theoretical ridge would give about 29.54 degrees. These are geometry checks, not a selected roof design: real tile/ridge build-up and eaves definitions must be allowed for.
- Keep the building as low as practical while meeting the applicable route, usable clear height, structural depth, roof drainage and the chosen clay pantile's minimum pitch. Product-specific tile suitability is still to be checked; flattening the roof without that check is not a solution.
- Three brick courses are preferred but may be reconsidered for height. Reducing the plinth does not automatically lower the ridge: the floor, frame and roof datums must move consistently, and moisture protection must still be designed. The historic 300 mm plinth assumption remains unverified.
- Set a coordinated target with allowance for construction tolerances after boundary distances and levels are known. Do not adopt 4.2 m, overwrite the preserved P02 model or issue a new numerical ridge prematurely.

## Can the drawings be produced here?

We can prepare the measured planning/design package: site layout, floor plan, elevations, relevant sections, dimensions, materials, coordinated schedules and editable sources. It can also form the basis of builder pricing and professional detailing, with provisional items clearly identified. Draft construction details can incorporate a competent designer's and engineer's specified information.

An architect is not automatically required simply to draw plans: UK law does not reserve architectural services exclusively to architects, although the title is protected. [Architects Registration Board](https://arb.org.uk/public-information/before-hiring-an-architect/who-can-use-the-title-architect/).

For this hand-built timber frame with clay tiles, the current illustration and preliminary drawings are insufficient as instructions to build. Before construction, a competent construction designer and structural engineer need to resolve or verify foundations/ground conditions, timber grades and member sizes, bracing, joints/connections, roof loads/build-up, stability, moisture details, floor levels and drainage. These roles can be coordinated with the builder; a full architect-led service is not the only arrangement. Their scope and appointments remain to be decided by the user.

Building Regulations are a separate check. Small detached-building exemptions have floor-area, use and construction/boundary conditions. At a 54 m2 nominal overall footprint, with a mixed enclosed/open arrangement, do not presume exemption or assess only the enclosed bay. Confirm the applicable floor-area treatment and approval route with building control when the detailed design is available. [Planning Portal building-regulations guidance](https://www.planningportal.co.uk/permission/common-projects/outbuildings/is-building-regulations-approval-needed-for-an-outbuilding/).
